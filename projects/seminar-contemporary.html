Two exam papers, written in response to set questions. The midterm uses Arjun Appadurai’s mediascapes and ideoscapes to compare how competing networks framed the Iraq War in Jehane Noujaim’s Control Room. The final traces how the meaning of nature shifted with industrialization, drawing on Walter Mignolo’s account of coloniality and Martha Nussbaum’s writing on justice for animals.

Midterm Paper

Question 4

Mediascape, according to Arjin Appadurai, is the “distribution of the electronic capabilities to produce and disseminate information (newspapers, magazines, television stations, film production studios, etc.), which are now available to a growing number of private and public interests throughout the world”, it is also the “images of the world created by media”. Ideoscapes on the other hand is the circulation of political ideas such as democracy, freedom and human rights. Mediascape and ideoscape both influence one another. Without mediascape, ideoscapes would not be widespread. Ideoscapes influence what media is shown. As seen in the Control Room, direct by Jehane Noujaim, the US military network and Al Jazeera both show different aspects of the Iraq War in accordance with their values. The US military network showed only parts of the war that would make them look heroic and patriotic. While Al Jazeera showed people who were affected and had sufferance because of the Iraq War. In this example, the US military network and Al Jazeera (mediascape) are influence by the ideoscapes of the US government (the military network) and AL Jazeera journalists. The US government wants to make themselves look good and justify their reasons for going to war. Therefore, the media that is presented to the people of the United States is a carefully constructed, filtered and politicized. The military networks do not show dead bodies or close up of killings and they always use captions that mislead or desensitize the suffering and actions of the Iraqi people. Because these American networks are presented as professional, factual and supported by the American government, people rarely question the credibility and truth of news before believing it. Since the publication of Arjun Appadurai’s essay, a new of media has emerged: social media. Now people are able to upload their own news and opinions on the internet. This new form of media has its own positives and negatives. A positive is that people (citizens, soldiers, journalist governments) are able to share news immediately in real time. Manu different viewpoints can be instantly showed online. However, a negative effect of this trait is that information is being showed online before it can be verified. Ethnoscape is the movement of people such as migrants, tourist and refugees. Technoscape is the spread of technology across borders. Financescape is the global flow of capital and financial markets. All five scapes are connected by they do not move together smoothly. The five scapes move in different directions and speeds by they are always overlapping.

For example, a black women face racism and sexist at the same time, not just racism right now and sexism later. A movement on gender equality might primarily focus on the acts sexism towards white woman and not the acts of sexism toward black and Asian women. An African American movement might not focus on the suffering of an African American women but primarily focus on the suffering of African American men. Even in the recent times, the majority of the deaths of citizen due to ICE reported in the media is primarily men even though an equal amount of women have perished in the hands of ICE. Question 2

Nature is the natural world around us. In metaphysical terms, it is the world created by God. However, during the Industrial Revolution, the meaning of nature was changed. The industrial Revolution was a period of time when human beings started industrializing and commercializing almost anything. The once beautiful landscape became a field of natural resources to plumage. Nature in Walter Mignolo’s words is “the source of natural resources (charcoal, oil, gas) that fueled the machines of the Industrial Revolution”1. Nature became something human beings could exploit for the purpose of fulfilling economic goals of the modern world. Anything and everything became something for human beings to put a price on and sell. Nature became closely related to economic success. Resources needed for everyday usage such as oil and water became popular commodities that make whoever that owns the are containing them wealthy. Because of this, many countries in the world have gone to war for the ownership and usage of these natural resources. Other resources such as diamonds, rubies and emerald became popular due to their rarity. As such, it became a symbol of wealth. The natural resources taken from nature is not just limited to inanimate objects, the term also includes animals and human beings. The colonization of Africa by the Western World produced a devastating product, the slave trade of the African people who were captured and sold to the Americas and Europe. Although slavery was not a new concept, the mass commercialization and shipping during the time period made it one of the most devastating events in history. The slavery of the African people shows that even human beings became natural resources. Another example of a living being treated as a ‘natural resource’ is Project Nim. Nim was bred in a research facility for monkeys, which he was sold only hours after being born. The people who bought Nim used him as a subject in an experiment that was made to see if animals could communicate to humans. After the failure of the experiment, due to Nim’s animal behavior, Nim was given back to the research facility, When the facility was under threat of closure due to a lack of funds, Nim and his fellow monkeys were then sold to another facility that conducted animal trials. The law then was that a pharmaceutical drug can only be sold on the market if it passed two animal trials. In this case, Nim became a resource to be exploited by humans even though he was a living and breathing beings. Later, he and his fellow monkeys became commodities for the ‘benefit’ of the human race to sell another drug to the world. Due to the over exploitation of nature and its resources, the world is running out of resources to give and is rapidly changing. It is only a matter of time before there is no coal left to mine, no oil left to pump and no gas left to extract. What will the humans do then. The Industrial Revolution waws not only the start of the exploitation of nature but it is also the beginning of global warming. The climate is change drastically due to humans. Tim Morton states “industrial capitalism has turned the Earth into a dangerous desert”2. It is only a matter of time when the Warth is too volatile to be able to live on and the human race will be perpetrators.

Question 4

Martha Nusshaum states “our imaginative sympathy with the suffering of nonhuman animals must be our guide as we try to define a just relation between human and animals.”3. It means the unjust treatment of nonhuman animals must be ratified before we can explode the boundary between humans and animals. Humans must first treat nonhuman animals as “beings entitled to a dignified existence”3. Human beings have the right to live with dignity; there is no moral reason human beings cannot allow nonhuman animals to live with dignity. All animals regardless of sentience have the right to a dignified life. What exactly are the requirements of a dignified life? All beings have different ideas and requirements. For humans its means having access to basic necessities and the ability to do whatever we wished. However, for animals it depends on their species. A predator animal such as a tiger or a lion needs to hunt prey animals for food. Their predatory capacities act as a survival skill and as exercise. IF the predator animal was denied this right, it would cause them suffering, the predatory act of predator animals also raises another question. Should predator animals have the right to exercise their predatory capacities if it means the loss of the life of the prey? My answer to this question is yes. It is the natural order of life and denying would cause the predator animal suffering. It is not as if the predator animal was using the prey animal as entertainment of causing it to live without dignity. A dignified existence for an animal means to live without suffering, to have basic necessities met and to not be exploited for the benefit of others, there are many situations in the world where animals are exploited and harmed by humans. An example of this is Project Nim. In which, Nim, a monkey, is used as a test subject for an experiment that was not beneficial to the human race but to answer a question caused by curiosity. He was then later given back oi the facility he was born in and the sold again along with other monkeys to be use in animal testing for pharmaceutical drugs. Another example would be circus animals that are taught tricks to bring entertainment to humans. Laws for animal benefits, such as banning the use and exploitations of nonhuman animals for the benefit of humans and the requirements of giving captive animals the required nourishments for a proper life, should be implemented. The size and significance of the nonhuman animal to human life should affect how they are treated and given a ‘dignified existence’. If the animal causes damage or suffering, the animal should be dealt with. Of course, this only applies to the individual animal and not the species. For example, if a mosquito sucked blood out of you a kept coming back for more, would you not kill it? If an animal got a taste for human meat and blood, would you not kill it for the safety for the humans living in the area? Of course, the non-harmful issues with animals should be dealt according to the situation. If a rabbit ravages your garden, there is no need to kill it, just build a fence around the garden. Nonlife-threatening situations should not result in the death and harm of the animal. Animals that are captive in zoos, aquariums or kept as pets should also be given the right to live with dignity. They should be given the proper nourishments and mustn’t experience suffering at the hands of humans. Animal rights have become a popular topic in the world in recent years. The cases regarding abuse of animals have surface and are being dealt with. With globalization, animal rights and suffering is being made aware of and laws are being implemented.
